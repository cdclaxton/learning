#Bayesian 

- Uses a variation of the [[Metropolis algorithm]]
- Can be more effective than samplers in JAGS or WinBUGS (especially for large complex models)
- Uses a proposal distribution that depends on the gradient of the posterior distribution
- **Potential function** -- negative logarithm of the posterior distribution
- Proposed next position is generated by giving a marble momemtum (left or right with magnitude sampled from a normal distribution) then allowing the marble to settle for a given (random) time
- Proposal is accepted or rejected according to the Metropolis decision rule, but with a momentum term
$$
p_{accept} = \min \Big( \frac{p(\theta_{proposed}|D) p(\theta_{proposed})}{p(\theta_{current}|D) p(\theta_{current})}, 1 \Big)
$$
- Parameters:
	- Step size, epsilon, eps
	- Number of steps
- Total duration of the trajectory = $\text{steps} \times \text{eps}$
- Look for an acceptance rate of about 65%
	- Too low -- reduce epsilon
	- Too high -- increase epsilon
	- Compensate with changes in the number of steps
- *No U-turn Sampler* (NUTS)
	- Estimates when to stop trajectories before they make a U-turn back to the starting position
- Standard deviation of the momentum is typically set by adaptive algorithms to match the SD of the posterior
- Gradient is calculated using symbolic math
